\section{Problem Statement}

Given a city modelled as an $n{\times}n$ integer grid, a set of
clients $J$ requiring wireless coverage, and a set of candidate
repeater locations $V$ with associated placement costs, we seek the
\textbf{minimum-cost} subset of locations at which to install repeaters
such that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every client is within Euclidean radius $R$ of at least one
        active repeater \emph{(coverage)};
  \item all active repeaters form a single connected component rooted
        at a fixed source node $v_0$ \emph{(backbone connectivity)}.
\end{itemize}
The problem is a variant of the \textbf{Capacitated Facility Location
Problem (CFLP)} augmented with a graph-connectivity constraint,
and is NP-hard by reduction from \textsc{Set Cover}.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Sets \& Parameters}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
\fontsize{7.5pt}{9pt}\selectfont
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
$V = \{0,\dots,n^2{-}1\}$    & Candidate locations; $v_0=0$ is source \\
$J = \{1,\dots,m\}$           & Client set \\
$E \subseteq V\!\times\!V$    & Undirected edges: $(i,k)\!\in\!E$ if
                                $d_{ik}\!\leq\!\max(r_i,r_k)$ \\
$A$                           & Directed arcs: both $(i,k)$ and $(k,i)$
                                for each $(i,k)\!\in\!E$ \\
$c_i \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$      & Placement cost at location $i$ \\
$R,\,R_S \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$  & Coverage radius (repeater / source) \\
$r_i$                         & $R_S$ if $i=0$, else $R$ \\
$\mathrm{cov}(i)\subseteq J$  & Clients within $r_i$ of location $i$ \\
$M$                           & Big-M constant $= |V|-1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Decision Variables}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
\fontsize{7.5pt}{9pt}\selectfont
\begin{tabular}{@{}cll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Var} & \textbf{Domain} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
$x_i$    & $\{0,1\}$          & 1 if repeater placed at $i$ \\
$y_{ij}$ & $\{0,1\}$          & 1 if client $j$ served by repeater $i$ \\
$z_{ik}$ & $\{0,1\}$          & 1 if undirected edge $(i,k)\!\in\!E$ active \\
$f_{ik}$ & $\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$& Flow on directed arc $(i,k)\!\in\!A$ \\
$s_0$    & $[0,M]$            & Slack supply injected at source $v_0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Objective Function}

\begin{equation}
  \min_{x} \quad \sum_{i \in V}\; c_i \cdot x_i
  \tag{P}
\end{equation}

Minimise total deployment cost. Post-solve profitability is reported as
$\pi_i = \bigl(\sum_j y_{ij}\bigr)/c_i$.

\subsection{Constraints}

\textbf{(C0) Source always active}
\begin{equation}
  x_0 = 1
  \tag{C0}
\end{equation}

\textbf{(C1) Full client coverage}
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i \in V} y_{ij} \;\geq\; 1
  \qquad \forall\,j \in J
  \tag{C1}
\end{equation}

\textbf{(C2) Assignment to active repeaters only}
\begin{equation}
  y_{ij} \;\leq\; x_i
  \qquad \forall\,i \in V,\; j \in \mathrm{cov}(i)
  \tag{C2}
\end{equation}

\textbf{(C3) Edge activation (tightened)}
\begin{equation}
  z_{ik} \leq x_i,\quad z_{ik} \leq x_k,\quad
  z_{ik} \geq x_i + x_k - 1
  \quad \forall\,(i,k)\in E
  \tag{C3}
\end{equation}
The third inequality forces $z_{ik}=1$ whenever both endpoints are
active, tightening the LP relaxation.

\textbf{(C4) Flow only on active arcs}
\begin{equation}
  f_{ik} \;\leq\; M \cdot z_{e(i,k)}
  \qquad \forall\,(i,k)\in A
  \tag{C4}
\end{equation}
where $e(i,k)$ maps each directed arc to its undirected edge.

\textbf{(C5) Single-commodity flow (connectivity)}
\begin{align}
  s_0 + \!\!\sum_{k:(k,0)\in A}\!\! f_{k0}
       - \!\!\sum_{k:(0,k)\in A}\!\! f_{0k}
  &= x_0
  \tag{C5a} \\
  \sum_{k:(k,i)\in A}\!\! f_{ki}
  - \!\!\sum_{k:(i,k)\in A}\!\! f_{ik}
  &= x_i
  \quad \forall\,i \in V\setminus\{0\}
  \tag{C5b}
\end{align}
The source $v_0$ injects one unit of flow per active node; every active
repeater $i$ absorbs exactly one unit. An active repeater can receive
flow only through an active edge, so any disconnected component cannot
balance its flow, making the constraint infeasible unless all active
nodes are reachable from $v_0$.

\textbf{(C6) Domain:}
$\;x_i,\,y_{ij},\,z_{ik}\in\{0,1\}$;\;
$\;f_{ik}\geq 0$;\; $s_0\in[0,M]$

\section{Gurobi — Exact MIP Solution}

\subsection{Branch-and-Bound}

Gurobi solves (P) via \textbf{Branch-and-Bound} with LP relaxation.
Binary variables are relaxed to $[0,1]$ for a lower bound; fractional
variables are branched ($x_i{=}0$ / $x_i{=}1$); nodes whose LP bound
exceeds the incumbent are pruned. Cover, MIR, flow-cover, RLT, and
network cuts tighten the relaxation. Termination is at MIP gap
$\leq 10^{-4}$ (proven optimal). The instance yields
\textbf{803 variables} and \textbf{1246 constraints}
($n{=}8$, $|J|{=}15$); Gurobi explored 983 B\&B nodes and applied
249 cutting planes.

\subsection{Key Implementation}

\begin{lstlisting}
m  = Model("wifi_repeater")
x  = m.addVars(num_locs, vtype=GRB.BINARY)          # C0-C2
y  = m.addVars([(i,j) for i in V for j in cov[i]], vtype=GRB.BINARY)
z  = m.addVars(edges, vtype=GRB.BINARY)             # C3
f  = m.addVars(directed, vtype=GRB.CONTINUOUS, lb=0) # C4-C5
s0 = m.addVar(vtype=GRB.CONTINUOUS, lb=0, ub=BM)

m.setObjective(quicksum(costs[i]*x[i] for i in V), GRB.MINIMIZE)
m.addConstr(x[0] == 1)                              # C0
for (i,k) in edges:                                 # C3 (tightened)
    m.addConstr(z[i,k] >= x[i] + x[k] - 1)
# C5: SCF flow balance — one unit per active node
m.addConstr(s0 + quicksum(f[k,0] for k in in_arcs[0])
               - quicksum(f[0,k] for k in out_arcs[0]) == x[0])
for i in V:
    if i==0: continue
    m.addConstr(quicksum(f[k,i] for k in in_arcs[i])
              - quicksum(f[i,k] for k in out_arcs[i]) == x[i])
m.optimize()
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Metaheuristic — Simulated Annealing}

\subsection{Encoding \& Neighbourhood}

The solution is a binary vector $\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}$ with
$x_0=1$ fixed. Each neighbour is generated by flipping one randomly
chosen bit $x_i$ ($i\neq 0$), followed by a two-phase \textbf{repair}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Coverage repair.} For every uncovered client $j$,
        activate the cheapest node in $\mathrm{rev\_cov}(j)$.
  \item \textbf{Connectivity repair.} Run BFS from $v_0$ over active
        nodes. While orphan (active but unreachable) nodes remain,
        activate the cheapest inactive neighbour of any reachable node,
        growing the reachable component until all active nodes are
        connected.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Repair guarantees feasibility after every flip, so the best
recorded solution is always valid.

\subsection{Acceptance Criterion \& Parameters}

A move is accepted with probability
$\min\!\bigl(1,\,e^{-\Delta/T}\bigr)$,
where $\Delta = f_{\text{new}} - f_{\text{curr}}$ and $f$ is the
\emph{penalised cost}:
\begin{multline*}
  f(\mathbf{x}) = \textstyle\sum_i c_i x_i
    + 10^3\!\cdot\!\#\text{(uncovered clients)}\\
    + 10^3\!\cdot\!\#\text{(disconnected active nodes)}
\end{multline*}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Solution Quality}

Gurobi certifies an optimal cost of \textbf{36.89} (MIP gap 0\,\%).
SA finds a feasible solution of cost \textbf{42.10}, a
\textbf{14.1\,\%} optimality gap, using one extra repeater (14
vs.\ 13).  Both methods achieve full 15/15 client coverage.
The shared relay nodes (19, 27, 28) confirm that both approaches
independently identify the same mandatory backbone bridges.

\subsection{Speed vs.\ Optimality}

SA runs in \textbf{0.13\,s} versus \textbf{6.85\,s} for Gurobi — a
${\approx}51\times$ speed-up on this small instance.  Gurobi's
worst-case complexity is $O(2^{|V|})$; SA scales as
$O(T_{\max}\!\cdot\!|V|\!\cdot\!|J|)$ and is therefore better suited
to larger grids where B\&B becomes intractable. For $|V|=64$ and 15
clients, however, the exact solver is already fast enough that SA's
speed advantage is of limited practical value.

\subsection{Relay Node Cost}

The extra cost in the SA solution (42.10 vs.\ 36.89) stems primarily
from node 41 (cost 6.51), which Gurobi avoids by routing connectivity
through the cheaper pair 50+53 (costs 5.14+5.23, shared with client
coverage). SA's greedy repair activates the cheapest \emph{inactive
frontier} node at each step, which can miss globally cheaper relay
paths.

\subsection{SA Limitations \& Improvements}

The repair-based neighbourhood guarantees feasibility but can
over-activate nodes: each bridge insertion is a local greedy choice
with no backtracking. Potential improvements include: (i) a 2-opt swap
operator (exchange two active nodes) to escape local minima; (ii)
adaptive reheating when no improving move is found; (iii) longer
cooling schedules or larger $T_0$ to broaden early exploration.  Despite
these limitations, SA produces a valid, near-optimal solution at a
fraction of the exact solver's runtime, making it a practical baseline
for larger or time-constrained instances.
