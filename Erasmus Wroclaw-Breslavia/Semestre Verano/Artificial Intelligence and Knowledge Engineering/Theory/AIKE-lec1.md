Maciej Piasecki, G4.19, Department of AI, WUST  Artificial Intelligence and Knowledge Engineering   13  Ways   of   AI  Two defining perspectives  construction of systems or models mimicking the human brain or way of  thinking  development of systems or algorithms that express ability to solve problems  difficult to humans with performance approaching the one of humans  “The two schools of thought” (Aggarwal, 2021: pp. 2)  deduction vs induction  deduction: from the general to the specific, from rules (knowledge) and facts to  conclusions, e.g.  All canine animals have four legs. All dogs are canines.   ⟹   Therefore, dogs have  four legs.  induction: from the specific to the general, a kind of ‘faulty’ logic — generalising  from a limited number of examples, e.g.  Tomasz is a student. Dominik is a student. Both are well prepared for a laboratory  class.   ⟹   Therefore, all students are well prepared for the class.  reasoning vs learning  reasoning: knowledge base   ⟹   chain of assertions   ⟹   true conclusions  learning: statistical inference (generalisation, induction)   ⟹   rules  e.g.   thousands of sea creatures lay eggs   ⟹   all sea creatures are most likely  to lay eggs   (what about whales! dolphins! seals! etc.)
